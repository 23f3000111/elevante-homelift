Website Development Brief
Elevante Homelift – Consumer Website
1. Background
The Elevante Homelift is a new type of homelift developed specifically by Elevante Beheer BV (EB) for existing private homes. The product addresses a simple but increasingly important problem: homeowners want to remain living comfortably and safely in their own home when using the stairs becomes difficult.
Conventional solutions generally force a compromise. A stairlift leaves the staircase in place but adds a highly visible mobility aid to it. A conventional homelift requires a separate position in the house and therefore takes up additional floor space. In many existing homes, finding such a position is difficult or impossible.

EB approaches the problem differently.
The Elevante Homelift is an integrated system in which the staircase and lift are designed to work together. The lift cabin travels in the space underneath the staircase. This makes it possible to install a homelift in existing houses where the available space would otherwise make a conventional lift difficult to accommodate.

The product should therefore not be presented simply as another lift. Its significance lies in the combination of comfortable and safe access between floors + integration into the existing home + efficient use of space + architectural/design quality.

EB owns and controls the Elevante Homelift brand and intellectual property. The product is being developed and industrialised with Doppler in Greece. EB is responsible for the consumer brand and B2C marketing. Sales, installation and local customer contact will ultimately take place through selected resellers/dealers.

The website will therefore be the central consumer-facing platform for the Elevante brand.

2. Product
The Elevante Homelift combines a staircase and lift into one integrated residential system.
The lift cabin moves vertically through the space underneath the staircase. When the lift is not at a particular level, the openings are protected by automatic doors. At the upper floor, an automatic door closes the stair opening when the cabin is elsewhere. At the lower level, the opening is similarly closed when the cabin moves upwards.

The system is intended to provide comfortable and safe movement between floors while retaining the staircase as a normal part of the house. The cabin is designed to accommodate normal everyday requirements, including use with:

?a rollator;
?a wheelchair;
?another person providing assistance.

Safety is an inherent part of the system and should be communicated clearly without turning the website into a medical or technical-safety website. For example, the system detects movement on or around the staircase: the lift will not start when movement is detected and will stop immediately if movement is detected during travel.

The product also offers choices in finishes, materials and cabin design. These design possibilities are important because Elevante is intended to become part of the interior of the home rather than appear as an added mobility device.
Detailed dimensions, configurations, finishes, technical specifications and certification information will be provided separately and should be incorporated into the appropriate product and technical pages.

3. Product-Market Combination
The primary product-market combination is: A premium homelift solution for owners of existing multi-storey private homes who want to continue living comfortably and safely in their own home.

The core market is not institutional care, hospitals, nursing homes or public buildings. This is fundamentally a private residential product.

The initial geographical focus is NW-Europe, first the Netherlands, followed by markets including the UK and Germany. The website architecture should therefore be designed from the outset so that additional countries, languages, dealers and regional content can be added without rebuilding the site.

The commercial model is dealer-based. Elevante creates consumer awareness and generates demand. Consumers are then directed to authorised local dealers/installers who handle the commercial process, site assessment, quotation, installation and subsequent service. This distinction should be reflected in the website architecture. The Elevante website must remain the authoritative source for the brand, product, design and consumer proposition, while local dealer information and lead routing support the sales process.

4. Target Audience
The principal end user is typically an older homeowner, with the main user group expected to be approximately 65 years and older.
These homeowners may find climbing the stairs increasingly difficult or unsafe but want to remain living in their existing home.

The website should address people who:
?own a multi-storey home;
?want to continue using all floors of that home;
?have sufficient financial means to invest in a premium permanent solution;
?value their home and its interior;
?do not want their house to acquire the appearance of a care environment;
?are considering future accessibility as well as an immediate mobility requirement;
?may use a rollator or wheelchair, now or in the future;
?may occasionally require assistance from a partner, family member or caregiver.

The purchasing decision may also involve adult children, partners, architects or advisers. The site must therefore work equally well for someone experiencing the mobility problem personally and for someone researching solutions on their behalf. The customer should be treated as an adult homeowner making an important investment decision, not as an elderly, or a patient.

5. Positioning
Elevante should occupy a different position from traditional mobility equipment.
The primary message is:
Comfortably and safely remain living in your own home.
The technology is the means by which that promise is delivered. The innovation behind Elevante is the integration of the staircase and lift, but this should not displace the customer benefit as the principal message. The communication hierarchy is therefore:
1. Benefit
Continue living comfortably and safely in your own home.
2. Recognition
Using the stairs has become difficult or unsafe, while the customer wants to continue using the entire house.
3. Possibility
A homelift can provide access to the different floors.
4. Elevante solution
The staircase and lift form an integrated system.
5. Proof
The cabin uses the space underneath the staircase, retains the staircase, provides appropriate access and safety, and can accommodate a rollator, wheelchair or assistance.

This hierarchy should guide both the website design and the copy.

6. What the Website Must Achieve
The website has several functions.
1.First, it must make the product immediately understandable. Elevante is unfamiliar and cannot rely on the visitor already understanding the concept.
2.Second, it must create confidence in a new product and brand. Consumers considering a homelift are making a substantial and long-term investment in their home.
3.Third, it must demonstrate that Elevante is a considered residential and design solution, rather than mobility equipment added to the house.
4.Fourth, it must allow interested visitors to progressively obtain more information without confronting them with all technical details on the homepage.
5.Finally, it must convert serious interest into contact with an authorised installer/dealer, preferably including the opportunity to see and try an Elevante installation.

The desired customer journey is approximately:
Recognise my situation ? understand the solution ? see how it fits into a home ? understand everyday use ? see real examples ? understand installation ? establish confidence ? find/contact a dealer ? see and try the product.

7. Overall Website Approach
The website should be visual first and information-rich at deeper levels.
The homepage should not attempt to explain everything.
It should establish the proposition quickly and then allow visitors to choose how deeply they want to investigate the product.

We want a layered structure:
Homepage ? product/design/application information ? detailed practical/technical information ? dealer/contact.
The site should feel closer to a premium architectural or interior product website than to a traditional lift or mobility-aid website. The site must nevertheless remain extremely easy to use. The principal audience is older, so visual sophistication must never come at the expense of navigation, readability or clarity.

The developer should create a controlled component/block system rather than a collection of individually coded pages. New pages should be constructible from approved Elevante components while retaining consistent typography, spacing and visual identity.

Any paid plugins or recurring licences must be identified in advance, including annual costs.
Elevante must retain administrator access and ownership/control of the website, domain, hosting, analytics accounts and relevant licences.

13. Visual Direction
The desired visual identity is:
?premium, architectural, residential, restrained and contemporary.
?The product should be photographed and presented as part of the home.
We do not want the visual language normally associated with:

?healthcare;
?hospitals;
?nursing;
?disability equipment;
?stairlift advertising;
?generic senior-living advertising.

At the same time, we must not hide the reason people buy the product.
The website can show:
?younger and older users;
?a rollator;
?a wheelchair;
?assistance by another person.
The distinction lies in how they are portrayed.
People should appear as homeowners in their own environment. They should look credible for the target market, broadly in the 60–75+ age range in lifestyle imagery, rather than using obviously young models pretending to demonstrate accessibility. A wheelchair or rollator should appear naturally in the context of using the product. It does not need to become the central visual symbol of the brand.
Assistance can similarly be shown as one person accompanying another, rather than through overt healthcare imagery.

15. UX for the Target Audience
Premium does not mean minimalist to the point of inconvenience.
The principal users may be 65+, so the website needs:

?highly legible typography;
?sufficient font size;
?strong contrast;
?obvious navigation;
?large, clear interactive areas;
?simple forms;
?predictable navigation;
?clear back/close behaviour;
?no dependence on hover interactions;
?no unnecessarily complex animations;
?no tiny controls;

?no hidden essential information.
The site should feel sophisticated because of typography, imagery, proportion, whitespace and discipline, not because basic navigation has been removed. Accessibility should be considered from the beginning of the design and development process.

16. Tone of Voice
The communication style is a particularly important part of this project. The central principle is: Human through restraint, not informality. Use respectful adult language. The language should be:

?clear;
?concise;
?factual;
?calm;
?confident;
?respectful.

Every sentence should communicate something.
Avoid generic marketing fillers such as:

?“discover”;
?“experience ultimate comfort”;
?“designed with you in mind”;
?“at your own pace”;
?“we understand”;
?“a world of possibilities”;
?“seamlessly fits your lifestyle”.


We specifically do not want patronising language directed at older consumers.
?Do not tell visitors how they feel.
?Do not manufacture empathy.
?Do not obscure simple facts with lifestyle language.
?If the lift accommodates a wheelchair, say that it accommodates a wheelchair.
?If an automatic door closes the stair opening, describe that function.
?The premium character should come from clarity and restraint, not an abundance of adjectives.

28. The standard against which the website should be judged
A visitor arriving on the website for the first time should be able to answer five questions quickly:
1.What does Elevante solve for me?
It allows me to continue living comfortably and safely in my multi-storey home when using the stairs becomes difficult.
2.What is different about it?
The homelift and staircase form an integrated system.
3.How can that work in my house?
The lift cabin uses the space underneath the staircase, making the concept particularly relevant to existing homes.
4.Can I use it in my circumstances?
The website provides concrete information about everyday use, including rollator, wheelchair and assisted use, installation and technical requirements.
5.What do I do next?
Find an authorised dealer, request further information or arrange to see and try the Elevante Homelift.
If those five questions are answered clearly while the website still feels like a premium residential design brand rather than a mobility-equipment supplier, the fundamental brief has been achieved.
